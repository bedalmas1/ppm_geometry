\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related}

\paragraph{PPM models.}
Deep next-activity prediction began with recurrent
models~\cite{evermann2017deep,tax2017lstm}. It then moved to stacked
LSTMs~\cite{camargo2019lstm}, CNNs~\cite{dimauro2019inception},
self-attention~\cite{bukhsh2021processtransformer} and
GNNs~\cite{dissegna2025gnn,wang2025rlhgnn}. Suffix prediction uses adversarial
LSTM encoder--decoders~\cite{taymouri2021mlmme}, direct
LSTMs~\cite{gunnarsson2023crtp}, Transformer encoder--decoders~\cite{wuyts2024sutran}
and fine-tuned language models~\cite{pasquadibisceglie2024lupin}. Benchmarks
show that reported gains depend on preprocessing and
splits~\cite{rama2022review} and that common splits leak
information~\cite{weytjens2022leakage}, which motivates our single
chronological pipeline. None of these works examines what the internal
representation contains.

\paragraph{Representations and reliability in PPM.}
Representation learning in process mining targets reusable embeddings for
downstream tasks~\cite{dekoninck2018act2vec,teinemaa2019outcome}.
Uncertainty-aware PPM estimates reliability from the output
distribution~\cite{weytjens2022uncertainty}. We instead measure the
representation itself and test whether it adds error information beyond the
output distribution (RQ2).

\paragraph{Representation geometry.}
Straightening of temporal trajectories has been reported in human perception
of video~\cite{henaff2019straightening} and in language
models~\cite{hosseini2023straighten}. Intrinsic dimension tracks
generalisation in deep networks~\cite{ansuini2019intrinsic}, and
trustworthiness and continuity~\cite{venna2001neighborhood} quantify
neighbourhood preservation. We adapt these tools to event sequences in two
ways: we index trajectories by process progress, and we compare latent space
against the space of possible \emph{futures} under explicit control for
history.
